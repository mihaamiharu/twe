## Launch thread

**Post 1**

Manual QA to automation is not just learning Playwright syntax.

It is learning how to move from a requirement to a defensible quality decision.

I built a free AI-Assisted QA Workflow course for that journey.

**Post 2**

The workflow has seven checkpoints:

1. Requirements analysis
2. AI-assisted test design
3. Test writing
4. Playwright automation
5. Test execution and evidence
6. Failure triage
7. Quality decision and capstone

**Post 3**

AI does not replace QA judgment in this course.

The working rule is:

AI proposes → learner verifies → learner edits → learner justifies.

**Post 4**

You will work with a fictional scheduling PRD, a public approved practice target, a Playwright + TypeScript starter repository, and a seeded failure to investigate.

Everything runs locally on your own machine.

**Post 5**

The course is designed for Manual QA professionals and QA professionals transitioning to automation—not complete beginners.

## Weekly supporting posts

### Week 1 — Requirements

Before writing a test, identify facts, ambiguity, assumptions, boundaries, and risks. A requirement is not a test plan.

Reply with one requirement ambiguity that has caused testing confusion in your work.

Continue the workflow on TestingWithEkki: `/id/courses/ai-assisted-qa-workflow`

### Week 2 — Test design

Ask AI for candidate scenarios, then challenge the coverage. Keep the scenarios that connect to risk—not simply the scenarios that sound complete.

What scenario does AI most often forget in your experience?

Course: `/id/courses/ai-assisted-qa-workflow`

### Week 3 — Test writing

A useful test case makes the expected result observable. If another person cannot understand what evidence would prove the result, the test is not ready.

Course: `/id/courses/ai-assisted-qa-workflow`

### Week 4 — Automation

Automation is not the goal by itself. The goal is a reliable check that preserves the intent of the test case.

Course: `/id/courses/ai-assisted-qa-workflow`

### Week 5 — Execution evidence

“The test passed” is tool output. Screenshots, traces, logs, and observed product behavior help turn a run into evidence.

Course: `/id/courses/ai-assisted-qa-workflow`

### Week 6 — Failure triage

A failed test is not automatically a product bug. Classify it as a test, product, environment, or data issue using evidence.

Course: `/id/courses/ai-assisted-qa-workflow`

### Week 7 — Quality decision

The final question is not “Are all tests green?”

It is “What did we test, what remains uncertain, and what risk are we accepting?”

Course: `/id/courses/ai-assisted-qa-workflow`
